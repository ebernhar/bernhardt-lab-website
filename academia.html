Navigating a career in academia

Academia can be a wonderful place to work, but it has its issues. This page collects advice, perspectives, and the occasional bit of humor on questions that come up for lab members at every stage — plus a lot more at the Chronicle of Higher Education.

Graduate school

Why is graduate school so hard?

It will test your self-confidence constantly, through classes, qualifying exams, proposal and paper writing, and the dissertation defense. It's nearly universal to reach a point where you question the decision — and your own ability to make it to the finish line. A few perspectives that help:

  • Is a Science PhD a Waste of Time?

    Slate — a recently-minted PhD's take on why it's worth it

  • How Do Graduate Students Choose a Research Question?

    Stephen Jenkins — on finding the question that will guide your dissertation

  • How to Be a Good Graduate Student

    Sean Carroll, Discover Magazine — written for physicists, applicable to everyone

  • So Long and Thanks for the PhD

    R.T. Azuma — reflections on what he wishes he'd known starting out

  • The Importance of Stupidity in Scientific Research

    Martin Schwartz, Journal of Cell Science — feeling stupid is a good sign

  • Embracing Criticism, Fostering Feedback

    Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution — on receiving advice from advisors and reviewers

And keep a sense of humor about it: an illustrated guide to a PhD, the "Thesis Repulsor Field" cartoon from phdcomics.com, and the Lady Gaga–inspired "Bad Project" video.

Writing

Dealing with writer's block

Everyone struggles with the writing process — starting is hard, and knowing when you're finished is harder.

  • Writing Science: How to Write Papers that Get Cited and Grants that Get Funded

    Josh Schimel — the whole lab read this together; it changed how we think about scientific writing

  • Online Guide to Scientific Publication

    Commonwealth Foundation — pre-writing strategy, audience, and handling rejection

  • The Science of Scientific Writing

    Gopen & Swan, American Scientist (1990) — how to structure scientific prose

  • Finding the Pitch in Ecological Writing

    Cahill, Lyons & Karst, ESA Bulletin (2011) — balancing "sell" against substance

Collaboration

Trials and tribulations

Working on research questions as teams is necessary, and hard. "What is Research Collaboration" (Katz & Martin, Research Policy, 1997) lays out the costs and benefits. The most important habit: talk openly and often about individual responsibilities and authorship plans, before tensions build. "A New Standard for Authorship" by Paul Friedman is a good, simple set of rules of thumb — and see the lab contract's own authorship guidelines, linked from the Join the Lab page.

Work-life balance

Managing an academic career and a life

Often framed as a question for women in science, but one that everyone in academia navigates to some degree. The structure of a typical academic career makes it hard to find the right time to start a family, or to be both an involved partner/parent and a successful scientist — once you have tenure, academia offers real flexibility, but the path there has real constraints.

  • Women in Science: In Pursuit of Female Chemists

    Nature (2011)

  • Housework Is an Academic Issue

    Londa Schiebinger & Shannon K. Gilmartin, Academe Online

  • The Door in the Dream

    A 2000 book of conversations with women in the National Academy of Sciences — how varied the paths to prominence really are

Diversity & inclusion

What can you do to diversify the academy?

The most important work any of us can do is to learn about the challenges underrepresented groups face — we can't walk in another person's shoes, but we can listen and understand the systemic challenges women, people of color, and LGBTQ+ scientists encounter on this career path.

  • What Is Faculty Diversity Worth to a University?

    Patricia Matthew, The Atlantic (2016) — on the uncompensated "invisible labor" of diversity work

  • The Invisible Labor of Minority Professors

    Audrey Williams June, Chronicle of Higher Education

  • Diversify EEB & Diversify EEB Grads

    Networks connecting ecologists and evolutionary biologists with women and minority scientists in the field — useful for building more diverse speaker lists, award nominations, and reviewer and hiring pools

  • If There Were No Barriers to Men's Participation, We Would All Be Doing It

    Jeremy Fox, Dynamic Ecology — on being a male ally to women in ecology
